# Intake question bank

Ask one question at a time, starting with the one whose answer would most
change the design. Skip anything the materials already answer or that does
not apply.

## The core six (use when time is short)

1. What do you have so far, and is this new or adapted?
2. When it is over, what should be different for the audience or students?
3. Who will be there, what are they there to do (learn, judge you, decide,
   or stay informed), and how close are they to your field?
4. How long is the slot, and in what format and setting?
5. *(If adapting)* What has changed from the original, and what must stay?
6. How will you know it worked?

---

## A. Starting point

- **What do you have so far?** A topic, notes or research, an outline, an
  existing deck, or a recording with feedback.
- **What kind of session is this?** A professional talk, a class session, or
  training that needs both a communication outcome and a learning outcome.
- *(Adapting)* **Who was the original for, and what was it meant to do?**
- *(Adapting)* **What is changing?** Audience, purpose, length, course level,
  or format (live, recorded, or circulated deck).
- *(Adapting)* **What must carry over, and what are you free to drop?**
- *(Adapting)* **What worked or did not work last time?** Questions people
  asked, confusion you noticed, timing.

## B. End goal

- **When it is over, what should be different?** What should people
  understand, believe, decide, or be able to do?
- **What would they say or do that shows it worked?**
- **Why does this matter to them now?** What happens if they do not get it?
- **Is there a specific next step you want?** Or is shared understanding the
  goal? An informative talk does not need an action request.
- *(Lecture)* **What should students be able to do, given what material, and
  how well?**

## C. Audience

- **What is the audience there to do?** Learn from you, evaluate or score
  you, make a decision, or stay informed. If there are several groups, which
  one decides whether the session succeeded?
- **How close are they to your field?** Specialists in your subfield,
  experts in an adjacent area, informed non-experts, or novices. If it is
  mixed, which group matters most?
- **Name one term or idea you would have to explain to them.** This tests
  the answer to the previous question.
- **Who will be there?** Roles, number, and whether they chose to come or are
  required to.
- **What do they already know, and how do you know?** Distinguish material
  they have seen from skills they have shown.
- **Are there subgroups with different needs?** If you could fully serve only
  one group, which would it be? Who makes the decision, if there is one?
- **What do they currently believe or expect?** Likely objections from a
  thoughtful skeptic, or likely misconceptions.
- **How do they see you and this topic?** Eager, neutral, tired of it, or
  resistant.

## D. You, the presenter

- **What is your role and relationship to this audience?** Expert, peer,
  outsider, instructor, or presenting to people senior to you.
- **What do you want from this for yourself?** Feedback on work in progress,
  collaborators, visibility, a job, or a good score.
- **What worries you most about this session?** The answer often shows the
  real risk.
- **Any delivery preferences or limits?** Comfort with interaction or live
  demos, language, need for notes.

## E. Occasion and constraints

- **How long is the slot, including Q&A?** Could it be cut short on the day?
- **What is the setting?** In person, remote, or hybrid. Room size and screen.
  Recorded or not. Any accessibility needs.
- **Where does it sit?** Conference session, meeting agenda, course week, or a
  series. What comes right before and after?
- **Will the slides or recording be used without you?**
- **Must-include items, off-limits topics, required template, or organizer
  rules?**
- **When are materials due, and how much time do you have to rehearse?**
- **Who could act as a human fresh reviewer?** Someone who has not seen this
  material and resembles the audience.

## F. Evidence and content

- **What is your strongest support?**
- **What is the weakest point, or what don't you know yet?**
- **Which sources must you use or cite?**

## G. Lecture only

- **Where does this session sit in the course?** Previous lessons it depends
  on, and where students will use it next.
- **What evidence do you have of current understanding?** Earlier work, quiz
  results, common errors.
- **What task will show that students learned it, and how will they get
  feedback?**
- **Class size, modality, participation norms, and expected preparation?**

## H. Research talks (with or without judges)

- **What is new here, in one sentence a listener could repeat?**
- **Is this finished work or work in progress?** If in progress, what
  feedback do you want, and where in the talk will you ask for it?
- **Who in the room matters beyond the talk itself?** Possible
  collaborators, a host, a hiring committee, a future reviewer.
- **How much method does this audience need?** Enough to reproduce it,
  enough to trust it, or only enough to follow the result?
- **What will a specialist challenge first, and what will a non-expert
  misunderstand first?**

## I. Judged or evaluated sessions

Use for research competitions, thesis defenses, job talks, grant panels, and
pitch competitions.

- **Do you have the rubric or judging criteria?** If not, what do past
  winners or organizers say matters?
- **What are the rules?** Time limit and how strictly it is enforced, slide
  or prop limits, required sections.
- **Who is on the panel?** Specialists in your field, a mixed-discipline
  panel, or non-specialists. Do any judges know your work?
- **How does Q&A work?** Who asks, for how long, and does it count toward the
  score?
- **What are you compared against?** How many other entries, and in what
  order do you present?
- **Is there also a general audience?** What do they need, if anything?

## J. Success and follow-up

- **What can you observe during or after the session?** Questions, a vote or
  poll, an exit response, a decision, a follow-up request.
- **What follow-up is possible?** Email, next meeting, homework, office hours.

## Follow-up probes for vague answers

| If they say | Ask |
| - | - |
| "Students" | Are the students learning from you, judging you, or presenting alongside you? |
| "A seminar" | Is it a class session, a research talk, or a job talk? |
| "Judges" or "a panel" | Do you have the criteria, and what expertise does the panel have? |
| "Experts" | Experts in your subfield, or in your discipline but a different area? |
| "A general audience" | Educated non-experts or the public with no background? Name one term you would have to define. |
| "Everyone" | Who is the hardest person in the room to reach, and who matters most? |
| "Inform them" or "give an overview" | What should they be able to do or decide with that information? |
| "Get buy-in" | Buy-in to what specific next step, and from whom? |
| "They know the basics" | Name one thing you will use without explaining it. |
| "Cover chapter 5" | If students left with only one skill, which should it be? |
| "Make it engaging" | What should people be doing or thinking during the session? |
| "About an hour" | What is the exact slot, and what happens if it starts late? |
| "Show our results" | What should the audience conclude from the results? |

## Debrief questions (after delivery)

Ask one at a time, then update the brief and decision record.

- What happened compared with the plan? Timing, cuts, surprises.
- Which questions were asked? Where did people seem confused?
- What did people decide, ask for, or do afterward?
- *(Lecture)* What did student work show, especially from students whose
  understanding is least secure?
- What would you change before giving this again, and for whom?
