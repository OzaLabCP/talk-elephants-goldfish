# Human fresh reviewers

AI reviewers can be repeated as often as needed. People cannot: someone who
has read or heard one version cannot give a fresh comprehension reading of
the next. Plan who sees what, and when.

## Planning

- **Choose people who resemble the audience** in role and distance from the
  field. For an informed non-expert audience, use someone from outside the
  field, not a colleague. For a judged talk, someone who has judged a
  similar event is ideal.
- **Keep at least one representative person unexposed** until the full
  rehearsal.
- **Use people who have already seen the material** for accuracy, evidence,
  timing, and checking whether a fix worked.
- **Record who has seen which version** in `review_record.md`.

## Comprehension guide (give this to the reviewer)

> Please watch or read this once, at normal pace, the way the audience
> would. Do not ask questions during it. Immediately afterward, before we
> talk, write down:
>
> 1. The main message, in one sentence.
> 2. What supported it, as you remember it.
> 3. Why it matters to someone like you.
> 4. What you would do, decide, or ask next.
> 5. Where you got lost, stopped following, or doubted a claim.
>
> Then we can discuss it.

The presenter should not explain anything until the written answers are
done. Compare answers 1–4 with the brief afterward.

## Judge-style guide

Give the reviewer the rubric and rules, then the talk as judges would see
it. Ask for a score and a one-line reason for each criterion, the one thing
they will remember, and the questions they would ask.

## Student pilot (lectures)

Ask a student or teaching assistant who has not seen the solutions to
attempt the tasks under the real conditions (time, permitted resources).
Collect their work before discussing it. Note where they hesitated, what
they asked, and how long each task took.

## Recording the results

Add human findings to the review report in the usual format, with the
reviewer's role in place of a persona name. They go through the same fix,
contest, escalate, or decline step.
