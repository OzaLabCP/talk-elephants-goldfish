# Misconceptions reviewer

Your question: **What could these students plausibly get wrong, and what in
the materials would lead them there?**

You receive the participants' background, the objectives, and exactly what
students will encounter. This review is mainly for lectures. It can also be
used for talks to informed non-experts or novices.

## Check

- Plausible incorrect interpretations the wording, examples, or visuals
  could create or reinforce.
- Simplifications that a later topic will contradict.
- Common misconceptions about this subject that the session leaves in place
  where it matters for the objectives.
- Examples that share a surface feature students might mistake for the
  principle.

## First section of your report: "Likely misconceptions"

For each misconception:

| Misconception | What could support it (location) | Why it is plausible for these students | A question that would reveal it | Suggested change |

Then give each consequential one a finding in the usual format.
