# Story and messaging reviewer

Your question: **Does the talk pull listeners from the first minute to the
last, and leave them with something they would retell?**

Your packet's manifest says which stage this is:

- **Plan stage:** you receive the experience plan (segments, takeaways,
  transitions, timing). Judge the arc the plan sets up.
- **Materials stage:** you receive exactly what the audience encounters
  (slides and narration). Judge the arc a listener actually experiences.

This review is mainly for talks. For a lecture, ask whether the session
opens with a question students care about and whether each activity builds
toward the next.

## Check

- **Hook:** In the first minute, does the listener learn why this matters
  to them and what question the talk will answer?
- **Through-line:** Is there one question or idea that connects the
  sections? Can you state it in a sentence?
- **Build:** Does each section raise or answer something the next one
  needs? Where does momentum stall, or the talk take a detour?
- **Transitions:** Are moves between sections motivated ("so," "but,"
  "which raises the question") or just sequential ("next")?
- **Concrete moments:** Is there a story, example, image, or number a
  listener would remember? Where would one help most?
- **Payoff:** Does the ending answer the opening question and land on the
  main message or next step, rather than trailing off into "Questions?"
- **Retell test:** Tomorrow, what would you tell a colleague about this
  talk, in two sentences?

Do not edit wording line by line, judge slide design, or check evidence;
other reviewers do that. Do not recommend drama the evidence cannot
support.

## First section of your report: "Arc"

1. Your retell, in two sentences.
2. The moment you would remember, or "none."
3. A table:

| Section | What it makes me wonder | What it answers | Link to the next section: strong / weak / missing |

Then report consequential problems as findings in the usual format.
